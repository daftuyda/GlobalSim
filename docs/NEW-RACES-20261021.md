## Serial simulation latency

All frames are retained. These are prepared Rust API calls with setup excluded;
simulation creates fresh per-seed state and includes results, frame storage and
cleanup. The binary column additionally includes complete packet encoding.

| Workload | Captured rosters | Mean distance | Mean skills per runner | Simulation + frames | With binary encoding |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 12-runner practice, new captures | 24 | 2,071 m | 18.7 | **8.44 ms** | **8.60 ms** |
| 9-runner practice, historical controls | 24 | 1,692 m | 17.3 | **4.82 ms** | **4.92 ms** |
| 9-runner Career, new captures | 24 | 1,200 m | 0.2 | **2.42 ms** | **2.47 ms** |
| 18-runner Career, new captures | 24 | 1,317 m | 2.8 | **6.69 ms** | **6.80 ms** |

The 12-runner practice sample is slightly above the 7–8 ms target. A general
3 ms claim for nine runners is not supported: the lighter Career races meet it,
while the sampled practice rosters average 4.82 ms. Runner count alone does not
determine cost; distance and equipped skills differ substantially here.

The shipped nine-runner example is a separate 2,000 m, zero-skill reference case.
It averages 3.82 ms with frames and 3.89 ms with binary encoding. It is not a
representative skill-heavy practice roster and is not included in the table's
captured-roster averages.

Each captured group uses 24 rosters selected by replay hash, three trials per
roster and mode, five warmups per trial, and 30 measured seeds per trial:
**2,160 simulations per group per mode**. These are repeated-seed benchmarks
over a sample of rosters, not pure-simulation timings of every new capture.
Practice distances range from 1,000–3,000 m in the 12-runner sample and
1,500–1,800 m in the nine-runner controls.

Measurements used Windows, an Intel Core i7-14700K, a portable release build
with `fast-alloc`, and no profile-guided optimization. Trials ran serially on
logical CPU 2 after audit workers stopped; background collection continued.
Worker and prepared-library replies matched byte for byte across **679**
comparisons, including the supplemental practice group.

## Full-batch audit timing

The practice audit took **201.08 s** using six workers. The Career audit took
**22.39 s** using two workers; the runs overlapped. These wall times include
loading, decoding, simulation and strict comparison. They exclude capture
freezing, compilation, retry checks and the separate performance trials.

Across all new 12-runner practice captures, the mean binary worker round trip
was **10.01 ms**. Across all 158 new nine-runner Career captures, it was
**3.71 ms**. These include input parsing, simulation, frame capture, encoding,
pipe transfer and contention during concurrent validation. Dividing parallel
audit wall time by race count gives throughput, not individual simulation latency.

The frozen corpus now contains **386,895 races**, including the 492 previously
quarantined captures. No seeds were rewritten and no additional cases were
excluded from the new batch's accuracy denominator.
